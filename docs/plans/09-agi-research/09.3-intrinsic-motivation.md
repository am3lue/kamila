## Hypothesis (testable claim)

Adding a bounded *curiosity* signal — selecting which already-authorized tasks/goals to advance based on novelty and expected learning gain (`07.1` uncertainty) rather than only user-requested or priority order — improves the long-term usefulness of autonomous work without violating user consent, as measured by user-accepted proposals and verified outcomes.

## Prior art to survey

- Intrinsic motivation / curiosity-driven exploration (Pathak ICM; random network distillation).
- Empowerment and information gain heuristics in RL.
- Human-aligned autonomy: "ask first" vs "just do it" trade-offs (respects `06.3` propose-first default).

## Sketched approach

1. **Curiosity selector** inside `06.3-orchestrator`: among candidate plan steps/goals, add a novelty score (embedding distance to recently executed work in `07.1`).
2. **Budget coupling:** curiosity-chosen work still consumes the same daily action/model-call budget and still requires the existing permission gates; default remains propose-first.
3. **Measure:** does adding the novelty term change which proposals users accept/act on (acceptance lift) and does it surface issues/learnings earlier?

## Cost & risks

- Cost: small — an extra score per candidate; no new model training.
- Risks: curiosity = drift (work that interests the agent but not the user); overfitting to novelty junk. Mitigation: novelty is a *tie-breaker among already-planned work only*, never introduces new unsupervised actions.

## Go/no-go rubric

- **Go:** in a 2-week A/B on real usage, curiosity-informed ordering yields ≥ 10% lift in user-accepted proposals vs priority-only ordering, with no increase in denied/reverted actions.
- **No-go:** any regression in consent or an increase in reverted/destructive actions.
