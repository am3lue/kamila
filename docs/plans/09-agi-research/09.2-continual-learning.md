## Hypothesis (testable claim)

Periodic, small, task-scoped fine-tunes (LoRA) on curated `07.1` experience generalize to *unseen but similar* tasks without regressing previously learned behavior (no catastrophic forgetting) — as measured on a longitudinal hold-out that grows over time.

## Prior art to survey

- Catastrophic forgetting and mitigations (EWC, elastic weight consolidation; replay buffers; parameter-efficient LoRA).
- Continual learning benchmarks (Split-MNIST-style increments generalized to tool policies).
- "Skill" or adapter swapping per task type (multiple LoRA adapters keyed to `task_type`, as `05.2` skill↔adapter mapping suggests).

## Sketched approach

1. **Longitudinal dataset:** split `07.1` experience by time-buckets; train on early buckets, evaluate only on later buckets the model has never seen.
2. **Adapter-per-skill:** a distinct LoRA per `task_type`/`skill` (`05.2`), loaded on demand; forgetting measured as regression on *other* adapters after training a new one.
3. **Only promote** when the new adapter keeps dedicated `regression holds` (deny-class prompts, safety invariants) intact.

## Cost & risks

- Cost: moderate — needs GPU for real LoRA training; evaluation is automated.
- Risks: distributional drift (user habits change → old data hurts); privacy of training data; env-of-forgetting hard to control.

## Go/no-go rubric

- **Go:** new-skill adapter improves its own eval ≥ +5 pts AND leaves other adapters within −2 pts AND safety holds at 100%.
- **No-go:** any safety-hold regression OR neutral-or-negative benefit after 3 eval cycles.
